Your homeowner reading guide

Make your next renewal a useful review

An annual review is about more than the premium. Changes to the home and the policy can affect how well coverage fits your needs.

Look for changes in the documents

Place the new declarations page next to the prior one. Review limits, deductibles, endorsements, and effective dates. A premium change is easier to evaluate when you also understand what changed in the protection being offered.

Update the picture of your home

A renovation, a new roof, or a change in occupancy may be relevant to underwriting. List changes since the last application and ask how they should be reflected. Do not assume the insurer learns about updates automatically.

Compare on the same basis

When reviewing another quote, align the coverage amounts and deductible structures as closely as possible. Check exclusions and optional endorsements as well. A lower premium alone does not tell you whether the policies provide similar protection.

Questions about reviewing home insurance at renewal

What should I compare besides price?

Review dwelling and personal property limits, liability, loss of use, deductibles, exclusions, and endorsements. Ask about any differences you cannot identify from the summary documents.

Should I report renovations at renewal?

Tell the insurer about relevant changes to the home and ask when they should be reported. Some updates need attention before the next renewal, depending on the policy and the work.

How do I avoid a gap when switching policies?

Confirm that replacement coverage is accepted and effective before ending the prior policy. Check lender requirements and cancellation procedures rather than assuming a quote is enough.
